Key advantages

Massive bandwidth

Two 5 GHz allocations, 71–76 GHz and 81–86 GHz, give 10 GHz in total: far more room than the lower-frequency bands most satellites use today.

High-capacity RF links

Wide channels support multi-gigabit space-to-ground links, so more data moves on every contact.

Highly directional

Beams at these frequencies are narrow and focused, concentrating energy on the link and limiting interference with neighboring systems.

Strong frequency reuse potential

Narrow beams let the same frequencies be reused across nearby links and gateways, multiplying usable capacity.

Compact antennas

Short wavelengths allow smaller antennas for the same performance, which suits payloads hosted on partner spacecraft.

Proven in orbit

Space-qualified E-band technology has been demonstrated through Almagest’s Elevation-1 mission. Starlink uses E-band extensively in its feeder links; Blue Origin’s Terawave, for its gateway backhaul and TT&C; and AST SpaceMobile, for direct-to-user broadband terminals.

Works with optical

Optical: satellite → satellite

Laser inter-satellite links move data between spacecraft in orbit, where there is no atmosphere in the way.

E-band: satellite → ground

High-capacity E-band RF links carry the data down through the atmosphere to Almagest gateways.

Space Network

Our data relay network progresses from Phase I, where surrogate satellites carry Almagest E-band payloads for rapid data downloads to our global ground network, to Phase II, where Almagest’s own satellites are added to deliver full data relay and connectivity services.

Phase I · The surrogate modelBuild the network without building the entire constellation Almagest E-band payloads (E-Nodes) fly on partner spacecraft under Almagest’s SpaceNet-IOM ITU filing, with access to the global ground network. Partners gain a new revenue stream and E-band data transport; the network scales faster with lower capital.
Phase II · ScaleAdd Almagest-owned satellites Distributed E-Nodes on Almagest and partner spacecraft, joined by optical crosslinks, with adaptive routing, prioritized mission-critical data and reliable transport over a globally distributed ground network.

Ground Network

Global ground systems deployed in phases, with gateway divergence for redundancy, resiliency and optimized data routing, interconnected with major IPXs for direct access to cloud, telecom and internet backbones.
